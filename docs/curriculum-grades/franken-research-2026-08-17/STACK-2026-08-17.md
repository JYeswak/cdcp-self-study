### B. Uptime outage analysis 2026 (headlines only — not laws)

14. https://uptimeinstitute.com/about-ui/press-releases/uptime-announces-annual-outage-analysis-report-2026 — 13 May 2026 — **Confirms** 8th Annual Outage Analysis exists for 2026. Per-site frequency still declining (5th consecutive year) but **pace of improvement has slowed**. External/fiber/connectivity rising. Power still leading cause of *impactful* outages; dominant hardware: UPS, transfer switches, generators. Grid constraints + high-density as new pressure. Human-error *related* outages still led by “failure to follow procedures.” ~2/3 of *publicly reported* outages over 9 years are third-party (cloud/telco/colo). Full report is paywalled; 9-page exec summary exists.

15. https://intelligence.uptimeinstitute.com/resource/annual-outage-analysis-2026 — 11 May 2026, UII Keynote Report 201 — **Confirms** same key findings + one extra headline the press release underplays: **major fires have increased gradually; Li-ion in UPS is a contributing factor**, with the explicit caveat that build-out volume may explain some of the rise. **Kills** treating any single % as a course law — the page itself is a teaser.

16. https://datacenter.uptimeinstitute.com/rs/711-RIA-145/images/2026.AnnualOutageAnalysis.pdf — May 2026 (full report, fetched) — **Confirms method, not a cartoon.** Three *data sources* (Global Survey 2025 n≈800; Resiliency Survey 2026 n>450 operators; public-outage tracker 2016–2025/Q1 2026), not three *cause buckets*. Severity is a 1–5 scale, not power/cooling/people. Human error is **explicitly not counted as a primary cause**; it is a **contributor** (survey: 92% of respondents say at least a minor contributor to their most recent impactful outage). Figure 5 (Resiliency Survey 2026, n=403, “most common cause of any *IT-service* outage”): Networking/connectivity 23%, Power 21%, No IT-service outages 19%, IT system/software 18%, Third-party IT 10%, Cooling 8%, Other 2%. **Kills** the three-bucket “power / cooling / people” cartoon as the 2026 official frame. **Kills** using 45% or 92% as a law — different questions, different n, different years, survey self-report, public tracker biased to big consumer-facing events. Also **confirms** Uptime is *not yet* treating dedicated AI factories as a measured outage class (too new; they flag the research gap).

17. https://www.datacenterknowledge.com/outages/outage-report-ai-boom-threatens-years-of-data-center-resiliency-gains — May 2026 secondary — **Confirms** the AI/density/ride-through pressure headline and quotes Uptime that cooling ride-through is shrinking. Use only as a pointer back to #14–#16.

18. https://journal.uptimeinstitute.com/lower-density-brings-server-efficiency-and-cooling-gains/ — 29 Jul 2026, Daniel Bizo — **Confirms** the ride-through claim in Uptime’s own voice: low-density air halls had **minutes to tens of minutes**; **cold-plate DLC typically cannot tolerate loss of circulation for more than a few seconds**; immersion rides through **several minutes**. ### C. ASHRAE TC 9.9 thermal / liquid classes

19. https://www.ashrae.org/File%20Library/Technical%20Resources/Bookstore/Supplemental%20Files/Therm-Gdlns-5th-R-E-RefCard.pdf — 2021 book; card © 2021, 2024 ASHRAE (“Fifth Edition, Revised and Expanded”) — **Confirms the current public card.** No 6th-edition Thermal Guidelines book found as of 17 Aug 2026. Recommended (A1–A4): **18–27 °C** dry-bulb; humidity **–9 °C DP to 15 °C DP and 70% RH** (or 50% RH if coupon corrosion exceeds limits). Allowable: A1 15–32, A2 10–35, A3 5–40, A4 5–45 °C. **H1** (high-density air): recommended **18–22 °C**, allowable **15–25 °C**. Recommended ≠ allowable. Allowable is functionality/warranty envelope, **not** a reliability statement. Liquid Table 3.1: **W17 / W27 / W32 / W40 / W45 / W+**; **all classes min 2 °C (35.6 °F)**; number in the name is the **max facility water supply**. Typical plant: W17/W27 chiller + tower (± WSE); W32/W40 tower / optional chiller or district heat; W45/W+ tower or dry cooler / district heat. **Kills** “ASHRAE says run the room at 27 °C” and “allowable = recommended.”

20. https://www.ashrae.org/File%20Library/Technical%20Resources/Bookstore/Emergence-and-Expansion-of-Liquid-Cooling-in-Mainstream-Data-Centers_WP.pdf — ASHRAE TC 9.9 white paper (class rename documented here; also mirrored by Vertiv) — **Confirms** the rename: W17←W1, W27←W2, W32←W3, **W40 new**, W45←W4, W+←W5. Full-range performance required inside a class. Recommends **liquid-ready** future capacity in new designs.

21. https://www.ashrae.org/technical-resources/ai-data-center-framework/introduction-and-purpose — ASHRAE AI Data Center Energy Performance Framework, live 2026 — **Confirms** same W-class rename in an official 2026 ASHRAE page, with plant diagrams for W17/W27 vs W32/W40/W45/W+. 22. https://dallas-ashrae.org/images/meeting/041625/the_ashrae_thermal_guidelines_for_data_centers_____past__present_and_future.pdf — 16 Apr 2025 chapter talk — **Confirms** FWS W-classes vs emerging **TCS (technology cooling system) S-classes** (S30/S35/S40/S45/S50 in the slides) because CDU approach can put ITE inlet **3–15+ °C above FWS**. ## Facts we should stack into the NTM season

Short, sourced. No invented laws.

### EPI map (so the interview does not confuse badges)

- CDCP = 2-day **awareness** of the facility stack (power, cooling, fire, cabling, security, DCIM). 40 Q / 1 h / 27 to pass. Not the calc course. (#2)
- CDCS = 3-day **vendor-challenge + calcs**. Official public list includes: SLD, UPS parallel, battery-bank calc + vendor check, fuel calc, generator paralleling, psychrometric + CFM/CMH + capacity, EMF attenuation, gas-volume + hold time, plus “evaluate/select” liquid cooling for AI/HPC. 60 Q / 90 min / 45 to pass. Needs live CDCP. (#3, #4)
- CDCE = project lifecycle (SON → IPVT → DLP), not more Ohm’s-law. Needs live CDCS. (#5)
- Ops vs design vs audit are **different tracks**: CDFOS/CDFOM/CDESS; CDRP/CDMS; CTDC/CTIA/CTLA. (#1)
- CDESS is the public home of ISO 30134 / PUE-CUE-WUE language inside the EPI family. (#7)
- CTDC public syllabus already carries **µEDC + immersion** — C-era, not B-era. (#11)

### Ratings — three languages, do not mix

- **Uptime Tier I–IV** = topology performance (I basic, II redundant *components*, III concurrently maintainable, IV fault tolerant). Three **plaques**: TCDD, TCCF, TCOS. Design ≠ built ≠ operated. (#29, #30)
- **TIA-942-C Rated-1..4** = same four *ideas*, different standard, optional-but-normative-if-used rating annex. TIA’s own three certificates are Design / Facilities / Ready — not Uptime’s three. Never say “TIA Tier.” (#31, #32)
- **EN 50600 / ISO/IEC 22237 Availability Class 1–4** = chosen from a **business-risk analysis**, applied **per infrastructure** (power, cooling, cabling) + Protection Class for security. Sister standards, not a Uptime audit. (#34, #35)
- A site can be TIA Rated-3 and not Uptime Tier III, and vice versa. Stack that sentence.

### Thermal — 2021 book still governs; 2026 just renamed the water

- Recommended air envelope is still **18–27 °C** (A1–A4) at the **ITE inlet**. H1 high-density air is **18–22 °C** recommended / **15–25 °C** allowable. (#19)
- Allowable is “it will function,” not “it will last.” Power/thermal management may throttle inside allowable. (#19)
- Humidity is a **dew-point** problem first. 70% RH recommended only if silver/copper coupons stay under 200/300 Å/month; otherwise cap at 50%. (#19)
- Liquid **FWS** classes: **W17, W27, W32, W40, W45, W+**. Number = max supply °C. Floor = 2 °C for all. W40 is the new gap-filler. Higher W → more free-cooling / heat-reuse, less ΔT capacity per litre. (#19, #20, #21)
- CDU approach means **TCS inlet ≠ FWS**. Do not quote a W-class as the chip inlet. (#22)
- New builds should be **liquid-ready** even if first fit is air. (#20, #42)

### Li-ion — what a facilities pro must be able to say

- Installation code: **NFPA 855 (2026)**. Also name NEC 706, NFPA 1 Ch. 52, NFPA 110/111, NFPA 13. (#24)
- Test method: **UL 9540A 6th ed. (13 Mar 2026)** + **installation-level large-scale fire test**. Cell/module report alone is not an AHJ package. (#25)
- Hazard Mitigation Analysis is the default conversation, not a special extra. (#26)
- Public hazards: thermal runaway → propagation, **flammable/toxic vent gas / deflagration**, **stranded energy**, **reignition days later**, deep-seated cabinets. Water cools; gas may not. (#24)
- Uptime 2026 *notes* more major DC fires with Li-ion UPS as a contributor, and immediately caveats build-out volume. Quote the caveat or do not quote the fire rise. (#15, #16)

### Outages — 2026 posture, not a pie-chart religion

- Frequency per site still falling; **gains are slowing**. (#14, #16)
- **Contributor ≠ root.** Uptime refuses to code “human error” as the primary cause; it is in ~9/10 as a contributor, usually “didn’t follow the procedure.” (#16)
- For *data-center* impactful events, **power is still the lead primary** (UPS → ATS/STS → generator). For *end-to-end IT service* outages, **network/connectivity is at least as large**, and public trackers now light up **external fiber**. (#16)
- Cooling is **not** the 2026 volume leader — but density is shrinking the **time** you have when cooling fails. That is the new sentence. (#16, #18)
- AI factories are **not** yet in the measured outage tables. Uptime says so. (#16)
- Costs and third-party blast radius are rising even as site frequency falls. Survey cost bands exist; do not turn them into a course constant. (#14, #16)

### 2026 floor vocabulary (interview, not exam)

- **Neocloud** = GPU-rental cloud, often in colo or purpose-built AI halls, not a new Tier. (#38)
- **GPU colo / AI factory** = density + liquid + fabric, not “a really hot enterprise room.”
- **Behind-the-meter generation** + **interconnect queue** = the 2026 siting constraint. FERC is rewriting large-load / co-location / BTM rules in 2026. (#44, #45)
- **Thermal ride-through** at 40–100 kW DLC is **seconds** (cold plate, loss of flow). Air halls were minutes. Immersion is minutes. So CDU pumps and often facility-loop pumps go on UPS; thermal storage is a design choice, not a nice-to-have. (#18, #16)
- **Catcher** = one idle reserve block + STS, actives run full. **4N/3** = four live blocks sharing spare, ~75% load. Do not synonymise. (#40, #41)
- **CDU leak** = L2L isolation + dripless QDs + drip trays + point + cable detection + fail-safe isolation at rack/row + a rehearsed runbook. Redfish/OCP is how the CDU talks. (#42, #43, #46)
- **Liquid-ready rack** = manifold keep-outs, drip containment, QD convention, busway, HAC/wall-manifold interface (OCP Redmond/Deschutes is the public drawing set). (#42)

### PUE / KPIs

- The only PUE that survives a grown-up question is **ISO/IEC 30134-2:2026**: boundary, category, period, mixed-use, on-site gen, unmeasured energy. Design PUE ≠ measured PUE. (#36, #7)

## Folklore to kill (with why)

1. **“CDCP is the calc course.”** Official CDCP is awareness. Battery / SLD / CFM / gas / fuel / EMF **calcs are CDCS public claims.** (#2 vs #3)

2. **“Uptime still teaches a three-bucket power / cooling / people cartoon as the cause model.”** 2026 report uses a multi-cause primary list (power, cooling, network, IT, fire, suppression, security, third-party…) and treats people as a **contributor**, not a bucket. (#16)

3. **“X% of outages are power / human error — put it in the course.”** Different surveys, different questions, small n on some cuts, public tracker bias. Uptime’s own appendix says the public set is not representative. Headlines only. (#16)

4. **“Tier III = Rated-3 = Availability Class 3.”** Same four *ideas*, three different instruments, different assessment bases, different plaques. (#29–#35)

5. **“We have a Tier III certificate.”** Ask: TCDD, TCCF, or TCOS? Design can expire in two years; ops is a third plaque. (#29, #30)

6. **“TIA still says Tier.”** TIA-942-C says **Rated-1..4**. Rating annex is normative *if used*. (#31, #32)

7. **“ASHRAE 2026 rewrote the air envelope.”** No 6th-edition Thermal Guidelines found. 5th ed. 2021 (card revised/expanded 2024) still governs air + H1 + W-classes. 2026 ASHRAE pages **reuse** those classes. (#19–#21)

8. **“Recommended 18–27 °C means run at 27.”** Recommended is the *target band*. H1 is tighter. Allowable is not a reliability blessing. (#19)

9. **“W27 is the chip inlet.”** W-class is **facility water supply**. CDU approach can add 3–15+ °C. (#19, #22)

10. **“UL 9540A cell test = the BESS is permitted.”** 855-2026 wants 9540A **and** installation-level LSFT; HMA; spacing/suppression concessions are performance-based on that data. (#25, #26)

11. **“Gas system puts out a Li-ion ESS fire.”** NFPA public page: water / NFPA 13 is the cooling agent 855 requires; reignition and stranded energy remain. (#24)

12. **“Catcher is just 4N/3.”** Catcher = idle reserve + STS. 4N/3 = shared live spare. Different utilisation, different failure math. (#40)

13. **“100 kW racks still have 10–20 minutes of cooling ride-through.”** Uptime: cold plates → **seconds**. That is why mechanical is on UPS. (#18)

14. **“PUE 1.2, trust me.”** Ask 30134-2:2026 category, period, boundary, mixed-use, on-site gen. (#36)

15. **“AI factory outage stats are already in the Uptime tables.”** Uptime 2026 says they are not. (#16)

16. **“Human error is the root cause.”** Uptime’s 2026 method: contributor. Root stays UPS / STS / generator / config / fiber / etc. (#16)

## Open questions the two NTM panes should chase next

1. **CDCS calc worked examples (public only).** Official headlines confirm *that* they calculate battery, fuel, CFM, gas, EMF. They do **not** publish the formulae. Next: IEEE 485 / 1184 public methods, vendor battery S-curves, and a **self-made** worked set — never an EPI workbook page or a dump.

2. **ASHRAE Liquid Cooling Guidelines 3rd ed. / TCS S-classes.** 2025 chapter slides show S30–S50. Is that in a published book in 2026, or still committee? Buy/check the Liquid Cooling Guidelines edition statement.

3. **H1 vs W-class in a mixed air+DLC row.** How do operators declare a hall that is H1 air residual + W32/W40 DLC? TIA-942-C requires H1 for high-density *air-cooled* ICT — does that bind a DLC pod?

4. **NFPA 855-2026 vs data-center UPS Li-ion vs outdoor BESS.** What actually changes in a *computer room* vs a yard Megapack? Read 855 Ch. 9 vs Ch. 14 vs NFPA 75/76 intersection. Chase NFPA 800 draft status.

5. **UL 9540 vs 9540A.** 9540 = system listing; 9540A = test method. Interviewers conflate them. One clean sentence + when an AHJ asks for both LSFT *and* listing.

6. **Uptime AI-factory resiliency paper** (flagged as in-progress in the 2026 outage report). Watch for: training vs inference resiliency, on-site prime power operated by DC owners, grid near-miss / new grid codes.

7. **Catcher + single-cord GPU trays.** ABB says STS is an SPOF on single-cord loads. Are 2026 NVL-class racks dual-cord at the tray or the rack? Does colo sell catcher on A and 2N on B?

8. **Ride-through numbers we can defend.** Uptime: “a few seconds” (DLC) / “several minutes” (immersion). Vendor GB200/GB300 loss-of-flow trip times? Thermal-mass of a W45 loop vs a chilled flywheel — one back-of-envelope, sourced.

9. **ISO/IEC 30134-2:2026 mPUE + on-site gen.** How BTM gas/BESS/solar is counted so a neocloud PUE is not a lie. Read the 2026 PUE text (paywalled) or a national transposition.

10. **TIA-942-C normative rating tables.** FOTC lists the flip. We do not yet have the actual Rated-3 electrical/mechanical/telecom deltas vs B. Buy or library the C tables if the season will compare Rated vs Tier vs AC.

11. **OCP Deschutes vs colo liquid-ready product.** Which US colo SKUs actually implement Redmond keep-outs / 2 MW CDU / 4-makes-3 busway, vs “liquid-ready” as a brochure word.

12. **FERC large-load / BTM end-state.** Show-cause is 2026 process, not a settled tariff. Track PJM co-location options + SPP HILL 90-day path vs “we just brought a turbine behind the meter.”

13. **Contributor vs root in Joshua’s own war stories.** Stack the Uptime method onto one real incident: what was the *primary*, what was the *contributor*, what would TCOS have looked at.

14. **CDCP 2026 syllabus drift.** Official CDCP page now names BESS, microgrid, liquid, STER, AI-driven cooling. Confirm whether the **exam** moved or only the brochure — without dumps; use EPI’s public “sneak peek” / screencast only.
