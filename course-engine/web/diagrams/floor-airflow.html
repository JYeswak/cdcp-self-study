The baseline
Cold air reaches every rack inlet at the design condition, hot exhaust returns without mixing, and the pressure in the plenum (or the containment) is uniform enough that the worst rack is still inside the envelope. That last clause is the whole job.
How cooling health should be reported
As a distribution metric across the rack population — worst-rack inlet, or an index over all inlets — never as installed tons versus IT kW. The aggregate number is green in almost every incident on record.

The two defects that get confused, forever. Bypass is supply air returning to the cooling unit without doing work: it wastes fan energy, wastes cooling capacity and depresses the temperature difference across the coil. Recirculation is hot exhaust reaching an equipment inlet: it raises server inlet temperature. Their causes are opposite — bypass comes from too much supply airflow, misplaced perforated tiles, leaky penetrations and too-high tile exit velocity; recirculation comes from too little supply airflow, missing blanking panels, gaps between racks and short rows. They coexist in the same room, and the fix for one worsens the other: turning up supply airflow to rescue a recirculating aisle manufactures bypass elsewhere and hides the original defect behind higher fan power. Diagnose per aisle, never per room.
